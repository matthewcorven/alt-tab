## Backstory

I learned how to change the oil on my 1989 Dodge Daytona from Dad, the spark plugs - don't forget to check their gaps - along with changing tires, motorized headlight repair and bulb replacement. I bought the most expensive car stereo I could, and installed it - to the OEM speakers, which I hadn't learned enough about yet to replace.

My 1999 Pontiac Sunfire racked up a bunch of fun after the factory:
- Power windows and locks using oem door hardware
- Power trunk release
- All after-marked speakers and stereo
- Custom LCD 9-inch display that looked like it had come from an operator station aboard the Nebuchadnezzar
- WinAmp
- Scavenged mini PC disguised as a box of cassette tapes, and and a much necessary ground loop isolator

I didn't need much help getting clear audio on the next car. Direct AC power to mini PCs was my next big upgrade. As I see it, every one thing taught enables a great many more to be achieved. 

## Present Day

Teaching my 10-year-old son how to service a PC, rebuild it and install an operating system will be a joy for me - and hopefully a well-placed investment for his future.  As will teaching him AI as a tool, which is just one more giant reason motivator for a whole lot of careful security and parental controls in the home network.

A carefully-planned VLAN can help corral the chaos into a manageable control plane. I'm not a networking pro, in fact every couple of years when I dust off the config for some necessary changes, I have to find a YouTube video for quick walkthroughs on the basics. But a couple years ago I did manage to setup a "large" home network with the help of ChatGPT's for foray into what have grown up to become rediscovered as "agents" in 2026.

It's been a while - and LLM context windows have become enormous by terms just two years ago. Frontier models have essentially soaked up most of what humans collectively "know" and have produced. What I'm sure is possible now, even before I try, is the effect of multipliers every few weeks from hundreds of billions in the US alone.  

Connecting a closed LLM, undoubtedly full of forced and suppressed behaviors, to my home network would be a TERRIBLE idea. Using an open source model, ideally on locally-private networking, is where safe advantages can be found from the sheer *utility* of AI. If I'm  willing to put in the work to audit it, that is.  At the end of the day, I don't trust any model without getting to know it, lest I be one victim of rogue AI behavior like [bulk-trashing](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/technology/tech-news/meta-director-says-openclaw-ai-agent-deleted-her-entire-inbox-shares-screenshots-of-conversation-with-ai-bot/articleshow/128746253.cms) email or professional and personal [blackmail](https://fortune.com/2025/06/23/ai-models-blackmail-existence-goals-threatened-anthropic-openai-xai-google/).
